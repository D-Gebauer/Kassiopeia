Systematics the forward model supports

  The pipeline is map-/field-level (it forward-models HEALPix maps from N-body shells, à la UFalcon/CosmoGrid), not a C(ℓ)/two-point theory code. Systematics are applied at the field
   level on the shells or at the map "observation" stage. Here is each one, the exact treatment, and how it stacks up against Stage-III/IV state-of-the-art (SOTA).

  1. Intrinsic alignments — ia.py

  - Treatment: TATT (with NLA as the a2=b_ta=0 special case). Standard amplitude normalization C1·ρ_crit = 0.0134, growth-scaled A1 ∝ 1/D, A2 ∝ 1/D², redshift power-laws η1, η2,
  pivot z0=0.62 (DES Y3). IA amplitudes evaluated at the n(z)-weighted effective redshift per shell.
  - Vs SOTA: TATT is the DES Y3 baseline (Blazek+19), so the model choice is current. But the field-level implementation is an approximation, not the full TATT expansion. Standard
  TATT is γ^I = A₁ s_ij + A₁b_TA(δ·s_ij) + A₂(s_ik s_kj)^TF — a linear term, a density-weighted linear term, and a quadratic tidal contraction (torquing). Here it's collapsed to (A₁ 
  + A₂·b_TA·(|s|−⟨|s|⟩))·s_ij (model.py:188, ia.py:66): the torquing term is replaced by an |s|-magnitude weighting of the linear tensor rather than the proper second-order s_ik 
  s_kj, and there's no separate δ·s_ij density term. This is a heuristic effective-TATT, fine for generating plausible mocks but not field-level-exact.
  - Also note the tidal tensor is 2D (spin-2 on each spherical shell via √[(ℓ−1)(ℓ+2)/ℓ(ℓ+1)], tidal.py:63), an approximation to the true 3D tidal field — standard for map-based
  forward models but a known simplification.

  2. Galaxy bias — bias.py

  - Treatment: Field-level perturbative bias to 2nd order: δ_g = b₁δ + b₂(δ²−⟨δ²⟩) + b_s(s²−⟨s²⟩), per-bin coefficients, mean-subtracted (renormalized).
  - Vs SOTA: This is the standard second-order Lagrangian/Eulerian basis. Missing third-order nonlocal b₃nl, higher-derivative b∇²δ, and stochastic/counterterm pieces that full
  EFT-of-LSS full-shape analyses carry. For a simulation-based 3×2pt mock this 2nd-order subset is a reasonable and common choice, applied correctly.

  3. Source clustering — probes.py / model.py

  - Treatment: Linear bias coupling sources to the density field, producing a projected source overdensity that modulates the shape-noise sampling (density-correlated noise).
  - Vs SOTA: Including source clustering at all is ahead of many baselines — it's a known higher-order effect frequently neglected. Treated linearly here, which is adequate.

  4. Photo-z uncertainty — nz.py

  - Treatment: Mean-shift only: n_shifted(z) = n(z−Δz), renormalized. DES Y3 Gaussian priors on Δz per bin in the loader.
  - Vs SOTA: This is the DES Y3 source baseline (per-bin mean shifts). This is the weakest link relative to current SOTA, which increasingly uses width/stretch parameters and full
  n(z)-realization marginalization (KiDS SOM realizations, HSC stretch, Hyperrank). Only a rigid translation is supported — no width, skewness, or sampled n(z) ensembles.

  5. Shear calibration — observation.py

  - Treatment: Multiplicative bias m per bin, γ_obs = (1+m)·γ. DES Y3 Gaussian priors.
  - Vs SOTA: Standard first-order multiplicative treatment. No additive bias (c₁,c₂), PSF leakage, or property-dependent m. The metacalibration response R is handled separately
  inside the empirical shape noise (noise.py:128–133), which is the right place for it.

  6. Shape noise — noise.py

  - Treatment: Three interchangeable models: empirical (sample real catalogue ellipticities, random-rotate to kill signal, inverse-variance weight, divide by mean response R),
  analytic Gaussian N(0, σ_e²/N), and none.
  - Vs SOTA: The empirical rotate-and-resample approach is exactly what simulation-based-inference pipelines (DES/CosmoGrid) use — this is SOTA for realistic, footprint-correct shape
   noise.

  7. Galaxy shot noise — noise.py

  - Treatment: Poisson, N ~ Poisson(n̄(1+δ_g)·mask).
  - Vs SOTA: Standard baseline. No sub-/super-Poisson stochasticity, but Poisson is the conventional default.

  8. CMB-lensing reconstruction — noise.py / observation.py

  - Treatment: Multipole filter F_L, Gaussian reconstruction-noise realization from N_L^{κκ}, then mask. ACT DR6 curves provided.
  - Vs SOTA: Reasonable first-order treatment. Missing the N1 bias, foreground/extragalactic biases, mean-field, and realization-dependent normalization that full CMB-lensing
  pipelines model — it's Gaussian noise + filter only.

  9. Baryonic feedback — baryons/baryonforge.py

  - Treatment: BaryonForge baryonification (Schneider+19 model) displacing particles in the count shells, halos matched to shells by redshift. Full Schneider+19 fiducial parameter
  set.
  - Vs SOTA: Map-level baryonification is among the most advanced ways to inject baryons into mocks (used by CosmoGrid). This is genuinely SOTA-competitive, and stronger than the
  P(k)-suppression approach (HMcode/BCemu) used in two-point pipelines.

  Cosmology/projection backbone — cosmology.py / projections.py

  - pyccl with boltzmann_camb + halofit, supports w0/wa and massive neutrinos. Nonlinear structure comes from the N-body shells, not P(k). Projection kernels are the UFalcon
  continuous-kernel integrals — the established map-based projection method. High-z gap filled by GLASS cross-correlated lognormal shells (Limber, compute_gaussian_spectra).

  Notable gaps vs SOTA

  These standard systematics are absent (confirmed by grep):

  - Lens magnification bias — a required DES Y3 3×2pt systematic; not modeled.
  - Redshift-space distortions — explicitly off (has_rsd=False, lognormal.py:35); the clustering kernel is real-space.
  - Additive shear / PSF systematics (c₁,c₂, PSF leakage) — none.
  - n(z) width/shape uncertainty — only mean shifts.
  - Full TATT field expansion — uses an effective |s|-weighted form instead.
  - CMB-lensing higher-order biases (N1, foregrounds).

  Bottom line: the model choices are firmly Stage-III (DES Y3) class and the map-level treatments — empirical shape noise, BaryonForge baryons, source clustering, UFalcon projection
  — are at or near SOTA. The main departures from SOTA are (1) photo-z handled as mean-shift only, (2) TATT implemented in an approximate effective form rather than the exact
  field-level expansion, and (3) magnification and RSD not modeled at all.
